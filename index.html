A sample story

Written for Jake, age 6

Favorites: trains · his dog Biscuit · mac and cheese

Tonight's story
trains Biscuit mac & cheese

Jake was the kind of boy who noticed things. He noticed when Biscuit's tail wagged faster on walk days. He noticed the way steam curled off a bowl of mac and cheese like it was breathing. And he noticed, every single night, the sound of a distant train rolling through the dark — low and steady, like a lullaby the whole world was humming.

Tonight, Jake couldn't sleep. He lay in bed listening, Biscuit warm and heavy against his feet, the blanket tucked up to his chin. He counted the stars he could see from his window — three, then five, then seven — but his mind kept wandering.

"Biscuit," he whispered. "Are you awake?"

Biscuit lifted one sleepy ear.

"I keep thinking about stuff," Jake said. "About tomorrow. About everything."

Biscuit shuffled up and rested his chin on Jake's chest, big brown eyes blinking slowly. That helped. Jake closed his eyes and, without meaning to, started to drift — right into the best kind of dream.

He was standing at a station he'd never seen before, but it felt familiar the way dreams do, like a place you've always known. The platform was lit with warm golden lanterns. A train sat waiting on the tracks, its engine breathing soft puffs of steam into the night air. It was long and handsome, painted deep blue, with brass fittings that caught the light.

Biscuit was there too, sitting beside him in a little conductor's vest, tail sweeping the platform floor. "Ready?" Biscuit asked — because in this dream, of course Biscuit could talk.

Jake nodded and climbed aboard. The inside of the train was the coziest place Jake had ever seen. Soft seats, warm light, and on a little fold-down tray in front of him — a bowl of mac and cheese, perfectly golden, steam rising in slow, lazy spirals.

The train moved gently, rocking side to side as it rolled through a countryside painted in midnight blues and silvers. Outside the window, fireflies drifted past like sparks. Trees nodded in a slow breeze. Jake ate his mac and cheese. It tasted exactly right — the way it always did when his mom made it on cold evenings, when the whole kitchen smelled like home.

Biscuit sat beside him, chin resting on Jake's knee, and every so often Jake would reach down and scratch behind his ears. The train didn't rush. It had nowhere to be. It just rolled on, quiet and steady, through the soft dark world.

Other passengers were asleep in their seats — a bear with a scarf, a rabbit clutching a stuffed carrot, a small girl curled beneath a patchwork quilt. Jake watched them and felt his own shoulders drop. His jaw unclenched. The bowl was empty now, warm and done.

He leaned against the window. The lanterns dimmed a little. Biscuit exhaled a long, slow breath.

And the train rolled on, rocking gently, carrying Jake through the quiet night, all the way home to sleep.
